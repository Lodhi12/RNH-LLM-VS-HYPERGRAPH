The inability of the Indian forces to overwhelm Hilli Fortress is
a classic example of the inherent strength of the fortress defence.
Despite their complete preponderance in manpower, armour,
weaponry, and effective air support, the Indians were beaten by
the strength of the fortresses and the tenacity of the men who
were fighting. It was only on 11 December, when so ordered by 16
Divisional Commander, that the troops carried out a planned
withdrawal to Bogra to defend it against the advancing enemy.
Our troops broke through the enemy encirclement to reach Bogra.
33 Indian Corps was held up and failed to overrun our fortresses
in Dinajpur, Rangpur, and Bogra. Until the cease-fire, all the
fortresses were still containing the enemy onslaught. Those who
reached the objective were ejected by fierce bayoneting. Positions
lost were recaptured by counter-attacks.
Major-General (Retd.) Fazal Mugeem makes the misleading
statement that: ‘On 9 December, to the great relief of the enemy,
Halli fell in the fourth attack of the day. The officers and jawans
were so exhausted, that they could hardly move.” Hilli was not
captured on 9 December. It was vacated on 11 December on the
express orders of GOC 16 Division. There is a big difference
between the capture of an area by the enemy and the withdrawal
from one defensive area to another in consonance with an overall
plan to vacate a certain area to defend another area in the depth.
The Hilli position was occupied by the Indians on 11 December
when we vacated it, not captured on 9 December.
