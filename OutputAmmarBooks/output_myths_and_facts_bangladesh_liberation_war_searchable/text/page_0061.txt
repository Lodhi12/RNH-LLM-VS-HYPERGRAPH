On the morning of 12 April, Nixon met in the Oval Office with Kissinger and H. R. Haldeman, the White House chief of staff, to discuss the
developments in Pakistan. Both Nixon and Kissinger worried the United States could become involved in Pakistan’s civil war.
Kissinger said that if America supported the insurgents, “we get West Pakistan turned against us, and ... the Bengalis are going to go left
anyway.”
Nixon agreed: “If we get in the middle of that thing it would be a hell of a mistake.” In an oblique reference to India, he said: “The people who
bitch about Vietnam, bitch about it because we intervened in what they say is a civil war. Now some of those same bastards ... want us to
intervene here – both civil wars.”
Kissinger said India wanted the U.S. involvement “because they are scared to death of their own Bengalis. Deep down, the Indians don’t really
want an independent East Pakistan because within ten years of that the West Bengalis are going to start bringing pressure on them for autonomy.”
As things turned out, Kissinger could not have made a more fatal miscalculation.
