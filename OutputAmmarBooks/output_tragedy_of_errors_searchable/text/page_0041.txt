Causes of Alienation
The most significant effect of the geographical separation of the
two wings of Pakistan was the alienation of East Bengal from the
rest of the country. Many were the reasons put forward by the
_ Bengalis for_the bitterness they felt towards their co-citizens in
the west. Some were, of course, exaggerated to achieve the
objective of selfish individuals. A few were completely false and
part of a disinformation campaign. But none of the issues were so
great, in the beginning, to be unsurmountable.
What was needed was a clearer understanding of the unique ;
nature of Pakistan’s geography and its peculiar demographic ~~
characteristics. A sincere effort was also required, not only to
devise, but to implement, policies which could have removed the ~
suspicion and mistrust which had crept into the minds of the
people on both sides. The politicians, military leadership, \
bureaucrats and businessmen all shared this responsibility. «
Tragically they failed to deliver. Mistakes were made soon after
independence. Regional _ political leadeys and frustrated
intellectuals capitalised on them. Errors kept multiplying;
emotions kept rising; demands and their suppression kept
growing till it was too late to prevent the dismemberment of the
country aided and abetted by a hostile neighbour.
/
What were the main causes for the glienation? These could be
grouped under cultural, economic, political and military. Behind
some of them was a foreign hand initially hidden and towards the
final phase brazenly open.
Language Issue
A major factor which alienated the Bengalis from the rest of
the Pakistanis was the language issue. National authorities failed
to understand the deep attachment the Bengalis had for their
mother tongue.
