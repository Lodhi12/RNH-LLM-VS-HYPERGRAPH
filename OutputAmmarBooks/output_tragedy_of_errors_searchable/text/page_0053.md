He went a little further in following military traditions in the

would have gone unnoticed or perhaps even appreciated in a West
Pakistani cantonment in the early days of Pakistan but it must
have created an opposite effect in that small Bengali town.
On the 23rd of March, 1969, General Agha Muhammad Yahya
Khan, commander-in-chief of the Pakistan army declared Martial
Law. The units in East Pakistan moved out to enforce law and
order. A batman of a sub-unit, temporarily located in Dinajpur,
went over to the deputy commissioners’ residence-cum office early
that morning and asked the guard to pull down the Pakistan flag
as it would now fly at the major sahib’s martial law headquarters
in that city. The soldier thought that he was just doing his normal
duty but the deputy commissioner, a_ Bengali official, and his
subordinate staff must have taken this step as an act of high
handedness by the ‘Punjabi dominated army’. Needless to say the
flag was restored at the deputy commissioner’s house that very
day*!,
East Pakistani soldiers in various army units were often
addressed by their fellow soldiers as ‘Bengali’ rather than being
called by their proper names. All of these little irritants, none of
them deliberate, when taken collectively, did convey, the
impression of a contemptuous attitude of the West Pakistanis
towards the Bengalis.
