The primary task of the army is to defend a country’s
international borders against any external threat. A subsidiary
role, which = certain circumstances assumes considerable
significance, is to act in aid of civil power to quell an insurgency
against the state, or simply to maintain law and order. Towards
this end operational plans to meet the former contingency and
plans for internal security (IS) duties to cater for the latter are
prepared, updated and kept ready.
In normal peace time conditions, when calm and harmony
prevails, the IS plans remain buried under heaps of other
administrative files in the adjutant general’s branch of any army
headquarters. But when the country is faced with internal
disturbances and the writ of the government-in-power begins to
be seriously challenged military commanders with foresight begin
to take a fresh look “at the dust-laden plans for assisting the
government to restore normalcy.
