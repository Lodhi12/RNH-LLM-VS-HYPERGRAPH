On December 7, fifteen nations, including Argentina, Burundi,
Cameroons, Ghana, Honduras, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Nicaragua,
Sierra Leone, Somalia, Spain, Sudan, and Tunsia presented a
resolution to the General Assembly of the United Nations.
Resolution 2793 (XXVI) called on the governments of India and
Pakistan to take measures forthwith for an immediate ceasefire
and withdrawal of the forces to their own side of the border. It
urged both countries to intensify efforts for the voluntary return
of the East Pakistan refugees and for rendering assistance to
them’.
Mr. Yakob Malik, speaking during the debate at the General
Assembly on the recently introduced resolution, repeated the
earlier stand of his government and insisted that a ceasefire must
be accompanied immediately by recognising the will of the East
Pakistani population. Huang Hua condemned India’s aggression
and the shameless support given to it by the Soviet imperialists.
Mr. Bush, while accepting that the crisis began as a result of the
use of force by Pakistan in March, emphasised that India has the
major responsibility for broadening the crisis.
Mr. Crowe representing the United Kingdom was non-
committal. He felt that bilateral consultations inside and outside
the Security Council could halt the conflict and help in finding a
peaceful solution to the desperately complicated situation which
gave rise to the outbreak of the war. Mr. Morizet of France
believed that the only realistic measure was to obtain a cessation
of hostilities without prejudice to what may be decided
subsequently.
