It is an irony of fate that the mover of the Lahore Resolution,
who in 1940 was considered to be a strong supporter of the
Muslim League, was expelled from its fold only a year later. The
Viceroy had asked the chief ministers of all the provinces to join
the Viceroy’s National Defence Council so as to help the
government of India in obtaining their support for the war which
Britain, along with their allies, was waging against Hitler. The
three Muslim chief ministers, Sir Sikander Hayat Khan from the
Punjab, Sir Saadullah from Assam and Maulvi Fazlul Haq from
Bengal joined the Defence Council without consulting the Muslim
League. Quaid-i-Azam was against the Muslim leaders joining the
Defence Council and asked them to resign. Sikander and
Saadullah, left the Council but Fazlul Haq continued to dilly dally.
He later did resign but also resigned from the Muslim League.
Fazlul Haq was expelled from the Muslim League for disobeying
Mr. Jinnah. He was blamed for "repeated betrayal of the cause of
the Muslims generally in India and particularly in Bengal"!*.
