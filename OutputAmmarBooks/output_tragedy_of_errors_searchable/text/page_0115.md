From the different versions given by those who were close to
the events it appears that by March, 1969 Ayub had reached a
decision to quit his office. The question as to how and to whom
power should be transferred must have been discussed between
the head of state and his service chiefs. Since the military
hierarchy had no confidence in the politicians and no faith in the
ability of a civilian set up to restore normalcy in the country ,they
must have come to an agreement that the army must stay in
payer but under a new leadership.
It is here where some pressure on the President by those
wanting to replace him became necessary. Ayub who perhaps
wanted to remain president though only as a nominal head of
state was told, in no uncertain terms that he too had to go, Before
matters could become worse for Ayub he adopted the only course
left to him.
Although the Hamood-ur-Rahman Commission Report blames
Peerzada and other principal staff officers at GHQ for forcing
Yahya to assume power Peerzada argues, with some justification,
that if at all any one was to blame for forcing Ayub to quit It,
should have been the service chiefs and not their staff officers.
