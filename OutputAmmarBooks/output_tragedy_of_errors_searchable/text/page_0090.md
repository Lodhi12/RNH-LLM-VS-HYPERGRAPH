East Bengal was the poorest part of the province of Bengal in
undivided India. It had the lowest percentage of urbanisation,
industrialization, literacy, electrical power Capacity and per capita
income at the time of independence. This was to be expected
during British rule as the rulers had no strategic reasons for
developing East Bengal. They concentrated on north-western
India because of a threat to their empire from that direction.
Whatever industries or business concerns existed prior to
independence were in the hands of the Hindus. It was they who
controlled the jute trade. Repatriating their earnings from the 900
million rupee worth of jute being sent to India. Major General
Ayub Khan, then commanding a division in East Bengal foun’ the
area ‘extremely underdeveloped’. The only good roads that existed
was, according to him, "30 mile stretch in Pabna district, a bit of
road near Dacca and Jessore and thats all" He goes on to blame
Maulvi Fazlul Haq and Hussein Shaheed Suharwardy who were
chief ministers of Bengal before Partition for_not improving the
infrastructure in East Bengal. "They did nothing to better the life
of the Muslims there" writes Ayub.
