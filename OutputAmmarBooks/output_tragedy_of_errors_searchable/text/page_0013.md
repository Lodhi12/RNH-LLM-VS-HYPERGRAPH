In 1974, the late Lieutenant General Agha Ibrahim Akram,
well known author of the book, Sword of Allah, told me about
writing his second book on the advent of Islam in Iran. On my
saying "why do you not write about the dismemberment of
Pakistan?" he replied "I do not write about defeat". I too felt the
same way, when it was suggested to me that I should write about
the Indo-Pakistan War of December, 1971, particularly because it
would mean treading on the toes of many of my senior colleagues —~
with whom I had developed a close friendship after retirement.
But then I recalled Brigadier S. K. Dalvi’s book the ‘Himalayan
Blunder’ where he had candidly criticized his own authorities for
the mistakes made during the Sino-Indian Conflict of 1962 in
which the Indian Army was humbled. The Indian leaders
benefitted by the criticism.
I picked up courage to try and analyse the events that led to
the break-up of Pakistan with no axe to grind. The dust-en-gulfed
NDC thesis was taken out of the shelf and re-read.
I was fully aware of the enormous difficulties 1 would face in
reaching the truth. President Agha Muhammad Yahya Khan,
Sheikh Mujib-ur-Rahman, Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto, General Abdul
Hamid Khan and Admiral 8. M. Ahsan had all passed away,
taking with them many facts, the disclosures of which would have
helped in unfolding the truth further.
Researchers and historians have to fall back on the written
word when they wish to carry forward their learned profession.
Nations must learn from past mistakes. Younger generations
must not repeat the errors of their elders. It is in this spirit that I
have chosen to carry out an independent analysis of the reasons
for the break up of the country. Nearly all those directly or
indirectly involved in the decision making process in the critical
years between 1968-1971 have been interviewed. Some several
times over and for extended periods of time.
