Strategic Orientation
The geographical separation of the two wings of Pakistan made
it virtually impossible to plan for a co-ordinated defence of the
whole of the country. The options available were either to raise
two self-contained independent armed forces, each capable of
defending its own area of responsibility against a numerically
superior enemy, or to provide for a minimum force in a less
vulnerable region with the major defensive strength lying in the
other. |
In the case of the former, the Indians would have been
compelled to pay a heavy price to achieve their objective of
dismembering Pakistan. Economically, however, such an option
could not have been sustained and the danger of the country
crumbling from within due to its people being deprived of the
basic necessities would have been very much there.
In the latter case one or the other wing would remain
dangerously exposed. Working on interior lines the Indians had
the ability of concentrating their armed forces against one flank at
Pane while denying that facility to Pakistan. Both the wings of
akistan had their strengths and weaknesses.
