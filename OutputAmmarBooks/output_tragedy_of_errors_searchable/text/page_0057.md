The One-Unit scheme was ill founded as it neither benefitted

designed more to prevent the domination of the Bengalis than to \°,”

‘In 1957, the East Pakistan Legislative Assembly re-affirmed
the demand for the Centre to have jurisdiction over three subjects
only viz:- defence, foreign affairs and currency. It should have
become obvious by now that the politicians and intellectuals in
East Bengal had begun to lose confidence in the central leadership
where they were under-represented, Being geographically
separated they felt the absence of authority to run. their own
affairs, in matters which concerned their social problems and their
economic betterment, more acutely than the provinces in West
Pakistan.
’ They genuinely felt that devolution of power, atleast to East
Pakistan, was necessary as inordinate delays were occurring in
solving routine issues which could be more speedily handled if the
powers of decision making was given to the provincial authorities.
Development of infrastructure, granting of permits and licenses, *”
sanctioning of loans, educational policies, raising of revenues were
in most cases being controlled by ministries located in Karachi.
This made it difficult and expensive for the provincial government
to fulfil the expectations and remove the grievances of their
people. Shifting the capital from Karachi to Islamabad caused
further resentment as it meant traveling another thousand miles
to pursue their requests. They also felt that the Punjabi influence
would grow even more with the capital in the north. "Did you take
my consent" asked Kondkar Mushtaq when this question came up
in the interview. "The selection of Karachi as the capital of v
Pakistan was not appreciated by the Bengalis" remarked Professor
Shamsul-Haq a former minister in the central cabinet. Enam
Choudhry, Secretary economic resources division in the
government of Bangladesh, also pointed to the shifting of the
capital to Islamabad as one of the factors in the alienation of the
Bengalis, .
