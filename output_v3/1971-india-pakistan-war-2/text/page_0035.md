India-Pakistan wars, became a recognized international border.  The Punjab, densely populated 
and cultivated on both sides of this border, had a relatively robust transportation infrastructure 
and offered some opportunities for mechanized forces.  However, its cities and villages could 
serve as ready-made strong points for a defender and its numerous watercourses, large and small, 
represented significant impediments to rapid movement.  The land grew increasingly arid and the 
infrastructure less sturdy south of the Punjab as the border passed through the great Thar Desert 
and ended in seasonal salt marshes barely above sea level at the southern extreme.
